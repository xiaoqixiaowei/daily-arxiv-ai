Abstract: Multimodal reinforcement learning (MRL) offers a promising paradigm for autonomous driving by jointly leveraging complementary sensor observations to learn effective decision-making policies. However, its effectiveness critically depends on temporal alignment across modalities, making temporal inconsistency a fundamental vulnerability. Even subtle misalignment or latency can corrupt fused representations and ultimately lead to decision failures. Existing studies have explored adversarial attacks to induce cross-modal temporal inconsistencies, thereby enabling systematic evaluation of the temporal robustness of MRL policies. However, existing methods remain limited in generality, realism, and adaptivity. To address these limitations, we propose the Split Temporal Adversary (STA), which formulates multimodal temporal attacks as a unified decision process that jointly optimizes attack timing and modality selection under realistic policy-aware and budget-constrained settings. STA includes two mechanisms: a) a unified multimodal temporal perturbation mechanism that delays observations via historical states, removing the need for modality-specific noise; b) a split multimodal adversarial decision mechanism that jointly optimizes attack timing and modality selection through adaptive optimization while leveraging the victim policy's clean actions to identify sensitive decision patterns. We provide a theoretical analysis of the induced distribution shift and a modality selection principle. Experiments in the CARLA simulator show that STA more than doubles the collision rate of the strongest baseline against PPO while using 17.8\% fewer attack attempts, and that it generalizes to a VLM-based driving policy. These results reveal temporal consistency as a critical vulnerability, highlighting temporal robustness as essential for safe autonomous driving.

Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models map visual observations, robot states, and natural-language instructions to robotic actions, but their multimodal interfaces introduce new security risks. Existing VLA backdoor attacks primarily study trigger-based activation and stealthy action manipulation, leaving conditional suppression of malicious behavior underexplored. We investigate suppression-aware backdoors, which combine conjunctive trigger activation with instruction-conditioned suppression. We instantiate this design through three variants: Cross-Modal Trigger, combining robot-state and spatially separated visual triggers; Self-Suppressing Trigger, requiring a state trigger and a [DISRUPT] instruction token for activation, with [RESTORE] token suppressing the attack; and Multi-Trigger, additionally requiring a visual trigger while retaining suppression even when physical triggers persist. We evaluate these attacks using SmolVLA on two SO-100 manipulation datasets against four baselines: State Backdoor, BadVLA, TabVLA, and SilentDrift. Self-Suppressing and Multi-Trigger reduce Isolation Forest detection from 96--98% to 1--18% and action-space l divergence by 10--14X, achieving 63--71% attack success rates with perfect clean-task performance. Experiments with OpenVLA on LIBERO further show that the attacks transfer to a 7B-parameter policy, evading STRIP and action-distribution-shift detection. Our findings establish conditional suppression as an important dimension for evaluating backdoor threats in VLA policies.
