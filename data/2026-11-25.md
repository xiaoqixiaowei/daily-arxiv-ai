Abstract: Adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar enables privacy-preserving, all-weather physical scene understanding across darkness, smoke, and walls. However, standard instruction tuning treats continuous radar streams as static text-like tokens, causing near-random global radar-to-text retrieval and severe physical drift under sensor blackouts. Conversely, coupling pairwise contrastive losses (e.g., SigLIP) directly into the shared backbone improves retrieval but injects $O(B^2)$ repulsive gradients that fracture continuous coordinate manifolds ($R^2 < 0$). We present PhysRF-WorldJEPA, a manifold-decoupled Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) that separates continuous electromagnetic state prediction from cross-modal language alignment. An unattenuated 1.0x clock anchor and parallel dual-scale RF encoder feed a lean ~3% LoRA LLM trained with two-step state-space EMA-JEPA (Denoise -> Rollout) and sample-axis Batch-VICReg to predict +3.0 s physical states; a stop-gradient alignment head then maps frozen prompt states onto the text hypersphere with false-negative masking. On a 4,541-window tri-domain suite (77/60.25/60 GHz) against 10 baselines, freezing the physical backbone and training only the decoupled head lifts R->T R@10 from 5.95% to 15.01% and improves MedRank from 107.0 to 60.0 while preserving identical physical readout: 3.37 deg ($R^2=+0.961$) blackout +3.0 s torso tilt, 1.89 m ($R^2=+0.679$; 1.19 m/+0.863 at the RF front-end) cluttered UAV altitude, 2.04 m ($R^2=+0.461$) behind-wall NLOS range, 0.9976 virtual-RIS invariance, and 100% fall recall. Across five 0.5B-1.8B LLM families (15 zero-overhead models), World-JEPA improves retrieval and 75%-drop completion in 10/10 runs and blackout tilt in 4/5 (Span-Euc), 2/5 (Scatter), and 5/5 (per-family best) over SFT.
